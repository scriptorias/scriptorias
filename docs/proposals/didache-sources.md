## 2. Editions in detail

### 2.1 Lightfoot & Harmer (1891): English translation (preferred)

- **Bibliographic record:** J. B. Lightfoot, *The Apostolic Fathers: Comprising the Epistles
  (Genuine and Spurious) of Clement of Rome, the Epistles of S. Ignatius, the Epistle of S.
  Polycarp, the Martyrdom of S. Polycarp, the Teaching of the Apostles, the Epistle of Barnabas,
  the Shepherd of Hermas, the Epistle to Diognetus, the Fragments of Papias, the Reliques of the
  Elders Preserved in Irenaeus. Revised Texts with Short Introductions and English Translations*,
  edited and completed by J. R. Harmer (London: Macmillan and Co., 1891). One volume, xii + 569 pp.
  Published for the Trustees of the Lightfoot Fund.
- **Didache pages** (from the scan's running heads): half-title p. 213; introduction pp. 215–216;
  Greek text with notes pp. 217–225; translation half-title p. 227; **English translation
  pp. 229–235**. The volume also prints the Latin *Doctrina Apostolorum* fragment with the Greek.
- **Scans:**
  - `https://archive.org/details/apostolicfathers00lighuoft`: Robarts Library, Univ. of Toronto;
    616 images at 400 ppi; marked NOT_IN_COPYRIGHT. **Primary.**
  - `https://archive.org/details/cu31924024254744`: Cornell University Library; 592 images at 300 ppi.
    **Second copy for proofreading.**
  - Later reprints (1898, 1912) exist. We cite the **1891 first edition** only.
- **Attribution:** Lightfoot died in 1889, before publication; Harmer "edited and completed" the
  volume. The translation is conventionally cited as "Lightfoot and Harmer". *Open question 1:*
  whether we credit the Didache translation to both, or to Lightfoot with Harmer as editor.
- **Rights:**
  - *US:* public domain (published before 1930).
  - *Life+70 countries (UK, EU, etc.):* Lightfoot (1828–1889) and Harmer (1857–1944) both died
    more than 70 years ago, so it is public domain.
  - A few jurisdictions have longer terms (e.g. Mexico, life+100). Harmer's contribution may
    still be protected there until 2044. Low practical risk; flag for review.
- **Suitability:** a careful, widely used Victorian scholarly translation. It sets scriptural
  quotations in italics, which we already reproduce. It has no verse numbers; chapters are marked
  as sections, so verse alignment is our metadata, as already designed.

### 2.2 Bryennios (1883): Greek editio princeps (preferred, under investigation)

- **Bibliographic record:** Philotheos Bryennios, *Διδαχὴ τῶν δώδεκα ἀποστόλων ἐκ τοῦ
  ἱεροσολυμιτικοῦ χειρογράφου νῦν πρῶτον ἐκδιδομένη μετὰ προλεγομένων καὶ σημειώσεων…*
  (Constantinople: Typois S. I. Voutyra, 1883). OCLC 24036753.
- **Scan:** HathiTrust `https://babel.hathitrust.org/cgi/pt?id=hvd.hnp4ei` (Harvard), full view,
  rights code `pd` (public domain worldwide by HathiTrust's determination). *To confirm:* whether
  a second independent scan exists; I found none on Internet Archive.
- **Pages:** *to confirm from the scan.* The text follows extensive Greek prolegomena, and
  Bryennios's notes are in Greek. He divides the text into 16 chapters. *To confirm:* whether
  he numbers verses at all; verse numbering is thought to come from later editors.
- **Rights:** Bryennios (1833–1917) died more than 100 years ago, so the work is public domain everywhere.
- **Suitability, for the reviewer to judge:**
  - Strength: it is the first edition by the scholar who found and read the manuscript, the most
    direct printed witness to Codex H54.
  - Caution: later editors (Harris 1887 with the facsimile, Lightfoot & Harmer 1891, Lake 1912)
    corrected or re-read some places. Our comparison will list every difference so the reviewer can
    decide whether we publish Bryennios as printed, or Bryennios with documented corrections.
  - Practical: the scan is Google-digitized (Harvard). OCR quality for 1883 polytonic Greek is
    likely poor, so this is effectively a manual transcription.
- **Alternative to keep in view:** Lightfoot & Harmer's own Greek text (pp. 217–225) pairs exactly
  with our English translation. Holmes's modern Greek text is a revision of Lightfoot & Harmer.
  *Open question 2.*

### 2.3 Lake (1912): Greek and English comparison

- **Bibliographic record:** Kirsopp Lake, ed. and trans., *The Apostolic Fathers*, vol. 1,
  Loeb Classical Library (London: William Heinemann; New York: Macmillan, 1912). Didache pp. 308–333
  (Greek and English on facing pages).
- **Scans:** HathiTrust `hvd.32044038455515` (the copy First1KGreek cites) and 13 more
  full-view copies (Harvard, Michigan, Rutgers), all rights code `pd`. Internet Archive
  `apostolicfathers01lake` is a 1919 reprint (Toronto); cite 1912.
- **Rights:**
  - *US:* public domain.
  - *Life+70 countries:* Lake (1872–1946) died more than 70 years ago, so it is public domain since 2017.
  - *Life+100 jurisdictions:* still protected until 2046.
- **Verse scheme:** Lake numbers verses. The First1KGreek encoding of his text has
  **16 chapters / 100 verses** (counts 6, 7, 10, 14, 2, 3, 4, 3, 5, 7, 12, 5, 7, 3, 4, 8). Our chapter 1 matches.
- **Existing digitization:** First1KGreek `tlg1311.tlg001.1st1K-grc1` (Lake's Greek), CC BY-SA 4.0.
  Use it for automated comparison only.

### 2.4 Holmes (2007): modern reference (consult, don't reproduce)

- **Bibliographic record:** Michael W. Holmes, ed. and trans., *The Apostolic Fathers: Greek
  Texts and English Translations*, 3rd ed. (Grand Rapids: Baker Academic, 2007). ISBN *to confirm*.
- **Access:** a print copy (library or purchase), or Internet Archive's controlled lending (needs a
  person with an account; one copy is `apostolicfathers0000unse_w6o4`).
- **Use:** check verse boundaries and record disagreements; cite for dating and context. Verse
  numbers and facts are not copyrightable; Holmes's text, translation, and notes are, and are not
  reproduced.

### 2.5 Other public-domain editions (for consultation)

- Hitchcock & Brown, *Διδαχὴ τῶν δώδεκα ἀποστόλων: Teaching of the Twelve Apostles* (New York:
  Scribner, 1884): Bryennios's Greek with translation. Internet Archive `teachingoftwelve00hitc`;
  Project Gutenberg #42053. Useful as an early check on the Bryennios text.
- Schaff, *The Oldest Church Manual Called the Teaching of the Twelve Apostles* (1885; 3rd ed. 1890).
  Internet Archive `oldestchurchmanu00scha`.
- Roberts–Donaldson translation (Ante-Nicene Fathers vol. 7, 1886) and Hoole (1894): possible
  future comparison translations.

**Chapter 16 (to explain in the overview):** H54, the only complete witness, breaks off in 16.8.
The *Apostolic Constitutions* (and, per some scholars, the Georgian version) continue beyond
it, which has led to proposals that the original ending is lost. The reader will show exactly what
H54 contains. The overview will present the lost-ending question as a disputed matter, with
sources.

## 4. Licensing recommendation for our own work (needs review)

| Material | Recommended licence | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Our transcriptions of public-domain texts | **CC0 1.0** (dedicate to the public domain) | They add no creative expression. CC0 avoids implying a claim over public-domain text, and maximises reuse and citation |
| Structured data: alignments, verse tables, metadata, manuscript records | **CC0 1.0** | Facts and reference data. Easiest reuse by other projects |
| Original editorial writing: overviews, notes, connection descriptions | **CC BY 4.0** | Reuse with attribution, without share-alike burdens on others |
| Site code | separate decision (currently unlicensed / all rights reserved) | business decision |

Alternatives: CC BY-SA for editorial writing (keeps derivatives open), or all rights reserved
(maximum control, less scholarly reuse).

**Existing repository issue (non-blocking, to fix in the pilot):** `content/_raw/` already contains
Wikisource and First1KGreek snapshots, which are CC BY-SA 4.0. Because the repository is public,
those files need a licence/attribution notice beside them. They may also be removed once our
own transcriptions replace them.
